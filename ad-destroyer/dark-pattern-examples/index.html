Dark pattern examples.
Bad design. On purpose.

Some interfaces help you choose. Others choose how to pressure you. Try six original demonstrations, see what they do, and compare a fair alternative.

What makes an interface a dark pattern?

A dark pattern is an interface choice that steers people toward actions they may not otherwise choose, often by obscuring information, making refusal harder, or applying misleading pressure. Persuasive design becomes manipulative when it undermines an informed, freely made decision.

Context matters. A genuine deadline or accurate stock count can be useful. A labeled promotion can be clear. The problem is deception, unequal choices, or unnecessary friction around an action the user wants.

How to respond to pressure online

  • Pause when a page claims you must act immediately. Verify the offer or warning elsewhere.
  • Read the actual button labels and distinguish the primary task from optional promotions.
  • Look for neutral decline, unsubscribe, and preference controls.
  • Leave a page that hides essential information or tries to shame you into agreeing.
